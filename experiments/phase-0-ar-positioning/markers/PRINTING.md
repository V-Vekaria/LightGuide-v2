# Printing the Phase 0 markers

Two markers are used:

| File | Role | Where it goes |
|---|---|---|
| `stand_marker.png` | the light stand being measured | rigidly on the light/stand |
| `reference_marker.png` | studio/subject origin | on the backdrop or subject position, facing the shooting area |

Both are generated by `generate_markers.mjs`. The copies under
`../android/app/src/main/assets/markers/` are the same bytes and are what the
app tracks — regenerate both together, never edit one by hand.

## Landscape, 4:3 - and that matters

The markers are deliberately **not square**. A square marker whose pattern has
no dominant direction can be matched 90 degrees rotated with equal confidence.
That was measured, not assumed: in the 2026-08-31 session roll sat bimodally at
0 and 90 degrees and flipped between consecutive frames, corrupting about a
quarter of all samples. A 4:3 frame makes a 90-degree match geometrically
impossible, and the solid wedge in the top-left corner breaks the 180-degree
case.

Print them **landscape**, wedge at the top left, label along the bottom. Do not
rotate them to fit the page.

## The size you print is a measurement input, not a preference

ARCore recovers the pose of an augmented image from its **known physical
width**. That width is what fixes the scale, so a print that is 5% small makes
every reported distance 5% wrong, uniformly. Getting this right is the single
cheapest way to avoid a systematic error that looks like AR inaccuracy.

Whatever width you print, enter the same number in the app under **SETUP →
stand cm / ref cm** and press APPLY.

## Print settings

1. Print at **100% / Actual size**. Turn off "Fit to page", "Shrink oversized
   pages" and any borderless scaling.
2. Use matte paper or card. Glossy stock produces specular highlights under
   studio strobes and modelling lamps, which is exactly the condition Phase 0
   needs to survive.
3. Print black-and-white at full contrast. Do not print a draft/toner-save
   version — feature contrast is what is being tracked.
4. Mount flat on rigid board. A curled or bowed marker biases orientation.

## Verifying the printed size

The width ARCore is told about is the width of the **whole PNG**, which
includes a white quiet zone that is invisible on white paper. Two ways to check:

Measure the **width** - the long, horizontal side. ARCore is given the image
width, so the height is not what you enter.

- **Corner ticks (preferred).** Each corner carries a small L-shaped
  registration mark at the exact image corner. Measure from the outer edge of
  one tick to the outer edge of the one horizontally opposite. That distance is
  the width to enter in the app.
- **Black frame.** The outer edge of the thick black border is
  **0.9219 x** the full image width.

| Enter in app | Black frame outer width | Whole sheet height |
|---|---|---|
| 15.0 cm | 13.83 cm | 11.25 cm |
| 20.0 cm | 18.44 cm | 15.00 cm |
| 25.0 cm | 23.05 cm | 18.75 cm |
| 30.0 cm | 27.66 cm | 22.50 cm |

If the measured frame does not match, do not "correct" it by lying to the app
about a nominal size — measure what you actually printed and enter that.

The app's default is 20 cm for both markers. Marker size versus detection range
is itself an open question (VAL-002); expect to print more than one size.

## Mounting

**Stand marker.** Attach rigidly to the light so the marker face points the
same way the light does — the marker's outward normal is treated as the beam
direction, so pitch/yaw/roll read as the light's aim. Any play between marker
and light head shows up directly as orientation error, so tape or clamp it to
the head rather than to a floppy part of the stand.

Mount both the right way up - wedge top-left, label along the bottom. A sheet
mounted rotated reports a constant offset in roll, and for the reference marker
it rotates the whole studio frame, which sends the left/up/forward guidance the
wrong way.

**Reference marker.** Mount vertically at the subject/backdrop position, facing
the area you shoot from, and leave it there for the whole session. It defines
the origin: `+X` right, `+Y` up, `+Z` out of the marker face toward the camera
position. Every saved pose is stored relative to it, which is what makes a pose
mean the same thing in a later session. Moving it mid-session invalidates any
target saved before the move.

## Regenerating

```bash
node generate_markers.mjs
```

Writes both the printable copies here and the app assets. The patterns are
seeded, so the output is reproducible; a marker printed today matches one
printed next month. Changing a seed or any drawing parameter produces a
different marker, which means every previously printed sheet stops being
recognised — rebuild and reinstall the app, and reprint.
